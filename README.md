We are land stewards. We plant trees, rebuild soil, restore water cycles — slow work, done in the open, owned by no one and benefiting everyone. This project applies the same ethic to our presence on the web.

Barlavento.eco is **not a platform**. It has no accounts, no algorithm, no content of its own. It is a **confluence**: the place where the streams of a bioregional community flow together and become visible as one watershed. Every story, every project page, every profile you find through this site lives somewhere a member actually owns. We aggregate; we never enclose.

> *"Be a landowner on the web, not a tenant."*

That line — from the open-web documentary *Code for the People* — is our founding premise. Most communities today live as tenants: their stories locked inside social platforms that can change the rules, sell the audience, or vanish overnight. A community that spends decades restoring land should not be sharecropping its own story on rented ground. The open web is a commons, like the aquifer under our feet — and commons survive only when communities tend them.

## What lives here

**🗺️ The Directory — who we are.**
Each member steward and project publishes a small, machine-readable profile *at its own web address* (following the open [Murmurations](https://murmurations.network/) approach pioneered in our [sovereign-org-profiles](https://github.com/ludwa6/sovereign-org-profiles) work). Barlavento.eco indexes only what members self-publish — so appearing on our map is always an act of self-description, never something done *to* you. The map grows the way a forest does: one tree at a time.

**🌊 The River — what we're saying.**
Members who publish do so on their own sites. Open syndication (RSS, with real-time push via rssCloud) carries each new piece into a shared river of news here. Readers see the whole community's voice; every piece links home to its author's own ground. Nothing is copied away from its source; the canonical version always lives with its steward.

**🌰 The Commons — how we build and decide.**
This repository. The site's code, the profile templates, the plain-language how-to guides, and the record of every decision — all public, all forkable. Community meetings remain where intentions form; this repo is where they become transparent, tracked work. If you can read this, you can audit us. If you disagree with us, you can fork us. That is not a bug; it is the guarantee.

## The growth path: Seedling → Sapling → Established

Not everyone in a community of land stewards is a web technician — nor should they be. Web presence, like a tree, has a lifecycle, and every stage is legitimate:

| Stage | What it looks like | What you hold |
|---|---|---|
| 🌱 **Seedling** | A page or subdomain here in the community nursery — we host it for you, you can take it with you anytime | Your identity, your words, guaranteed exit rights |
| 🌿 **Sapling** | Your own website, transplanted out of the nursery but still growing on managed ground (WordPress.com, micro.blog, and the like) | Your site and everything on it |
| 🌳 **Established** | Deep roots: your own domain, your own site, canopy self-sustaining — a fully sovereign presence no landlord can touch | Everything — the land and the tree |

One thing the metaphor teaches: **the land and the tree are separate.** Your *domain name* is the land; your *site* is the tree growing on it. You can claim your ground at any stage — a domain costs about the price of a bag of good compost per year, and it can point at your seedling page today and your established site in five years, without any link ever breaking. If you do only one thing after reading this page: **go claim your domain.**

The commons' promise is that the ladder is climbable. Templates, transplanting help, and occasional **site-in-a-day workshops** are part of what this project exists to provide. No one is left a seedling who wants to grow.

## Principles

1. **Of, for, and by its members.** Community meetings legitimate; volunteers execute. No role exists that the community couldn't route around.
2. **Landowner, not tenant.** Members' content lives at addresses members control. The hub holds pointers and excerpts, never the canonical copy.
3. **Opt-in by construction.** Nothing about a member is published that the member didn't self-publish. A profile *is* consent; absence is respected.
4. **Public by construction, never by filter.** Anything private stays structurally private — we never generate public views by filtering private content.
5. **Free and open source, all the way down.** Code, schemas, templates, decisions: public and forkable from day one. Portability is not a feature; it is the architecture.
6. **Subsidiarity.** Every job is done at the lowest level with the competence to do it well. The hub does only what member sites cannot do for themselves.

## Get involved

**If you steward land in Barlavento:** come to a community meeting — that's where this project answers to. When you're ready: publish a profile (we'll help), plant a seedling page, or claim your domain. Bring no technical skill; bring your story.

**If you're technically inclined:** this is a young FOSS project with real, unglamorous needs — plain-language guides, Portuguese translation, feed plumbing, accessibility. Open an issue or join a discussion right here.

**If you're anywhere else in the world:** everything here is designed to be copied. If your bioregion wants its own confluence, fork this repo and plant one. Tell us — we'd love a sister watershed.

## Stewardship and status

The `barlavento.eco` domain was purchased by the **Mud Valley Foundation (MVF)** and is held in trust for the community this project serves. Day-to-day stewardship is by volunteers, currently drawn from MVF, with the explicit aim of widening that circle; decisions of substance are brought to community meetings.

**Status: pre-launch.** The premise is agreed, the pattern is proven (the [Vale da Lama Journal](https://valedalama.net) runs the same sovereign-syndication stack one scale down), and the first version of this site will be a simple page and river, built in the open in this repository. Watch, star, or join in.
