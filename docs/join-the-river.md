# Join the River

**Who this is for:** anyone stewarding a project in the Barlavento community who has a website. No technical skill assumed. Nothing here requires a developer, and most of it takes under ten minutes.

There are **two separate things** you can do, and they are independent — do either, both, in any order.

1. **Join the River** — your news appears alongside the rest of the community's.
2. **Publish a profile** — your project becomes findable on maps and directories.

Neither one moves your content anywhere. Both are pointers back to your own site. You can undo either at any time by deleting one file or telling us to stop reading your feed.

---

## Part 1 — Join the River

### What the River is

A single page showing the latest news from every project in the community, each item linking back to the site it came from. Like a noticeboard that fills itself in.

### What we need from you

**One web address.** That's the entire ask.

Every blog or news section on the web quietly publishes a machine-readable version of itself, called a **feed**. It's the same articles you already publish, in a format software can read. You don't create it or maintain it — your website makes it automatically. We just need to know where yours is.

### How to find your feed address

**If your site runs on WordPress**

Your feed is your website address with `/feed/` on the end:

```
https://your-site.com/feed/
```

Paste that in a browser. If you see a page of text and your article titles — possibly looking quite raw and unstyled — that's it. That's correct, and it means you're ready. Send us that address.

**If your site runs on Wix**

Your feed is your website address with `/blog-feed.xml` on the end:

```
https://your-site.com/blog-feed.xml
```

If you see a wall of text with your post titles in it, send us that address.

If you get a "page not found," it almost always means **your site doesn't have a Blog section yet**. Wix only creates the feed once a blog exists. To fix it: in your Wix editor, add the **Blog** app from the Add panel, publish, and write one post. The feed address then starts working by itself. If you write news anywhere on your site — updates, events, announcements — moving that into the Blog section is what makes it flow into the River.

**If your site is on Squarespace**

Add `?format=rss` to your blog page address, e.g. `https://your-site.com/news?format=rss`.

**If you don't know what your site runs on, or none of the above works**

Send us your website address and we'll find it. If it genuinely has no feed we'll tell you what it would take — and if it's not worth it, that's a fine answer. A project with no feed is still a member of this community; it just won't appear in the River until it has one.

### What happens then

We add your address to the list. From that point, anything you publish shows up in the River within minutes, with its title, a short extract, and a link home.

**What we never do:** copy your article, host your images, or show your content in a way that competes with your own page in search results. The canonical version is always yours. Nothing is stored here that isn't already public on your site.

**To leave:** tell us, and we remove the address. There's nothing to delete on your end and nothing of yours is held here.

## Part 2 — Publish a profile

### What a profile is

A small file at your own web address saying who you are, where you are, and what you do — in a format that maps and directories can read. Publishing it is how you appear on the community map.

The important part: **publishing the profile *is* the consent.** Nobody adds you to a map. You publish, we point. If you never publish one, you never appear, and nobody will chase you about it.

### How to do it

The full step-by-step guide lives in our sister project and covers three routes, from "I have no website and no time" to "I run my own site":

**→ [Get Listed guide](https://github.com/ludwa6/sovereign-org-profiles/blob/main/docs/GET-LISTED.md)**

The short version: fill in a web form, get a file, put that file somewhere. The guide's Tier 0 gets you listed this afternoon with no website at all. Tier 2 puts the file on your own domain, which is what we'd eventually love — but starting at Tier 0 costs nothing and moving up later loses nothing.

If you'd rather not read a guide at all, say so and we'll do it with you in one sitting.

## Why we do it this way

It would be far easier — for us — to build one website, ask everyone for their text and photos, and put it all in one database. We're deliberately not doing that.

That approach makes every project a **tenant**. Your story would live on our system, at our address, under our rules, and if this project ran out of volunteers in three years, it would go down with us and take your work with it.

Instead: your content lives at your address, on your terms, and this site holds nothing but pointers. If we vanish, you lose a noticeboard. You keep everything that matters.

That's also why the ask is so small. One web address, and a file you control. If it ever felt like more than that, we'd have got the design wrong.
